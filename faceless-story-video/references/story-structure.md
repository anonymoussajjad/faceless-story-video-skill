# Story structure (moral / emotional, 60-90 seconds)

## The arc (every story, no exceptions)

1. **Hook (scene 1, first 3 seconds).** One surprising or emotional moment. Start in the middle of the action, never with "once upon a time". Example: a boy finds a wallet full of money on a rainy street.
2. **Setup (scenes 2-3).** Who is the character, what do they want, what is at stake. One character only — the hero. Keep the cast to one hero plus at most one background figure who never speaks.
3. **Twist (middle scenes).** The choice that costs something: return the wallet or keep it, tell the truth or stay silent. The twist is a decision, not an accident.
4. **Payoff (last 2 scenes).** The result of the choice, then the moral in one line. End on a warm image, not on a lecture.

## Duration math

- Total: 60-90 seconds. Shorter than 60 feels rushed; longer than 90 loses Shorts/Reels viewers.
- Scenes: 6-12. Aim for about 7-8 seconds per scene (60s = 8 scenes, 90s = 12 scenes).
- Narration per scene: 1-2 spoken sentences. A 25-second scene needs a paragraph (4-6 sentences) or it goes silent halfway — never leave a scene silent.
- Scene durations must add up to the total. Fix rounding drift on the last scene.

## Shot list (vary every time)

Rotate through `close_up` (face, feeling), `medium_shot` (hero + one object), `wide_shot` (place and scale), `full_body` (hero walking/acting). Never use the same shot twice in a row. Vary the place, the objects and the pose in every scene — same character, different picture.

## Camera moves

One per scene: `push` (slowly closer — for emotional moments), `pull` (slowly wider — for reveals), `pan_left` / `pan_right` (for walking or looking). Push on the twist and the payoff; pull on the first reveal of a place.

## Effects and ambience

- `effect`: `rain` (sad/serious), `dust` (warm/daytime), `embers` (hope/evening). One effect per scene, matching the mood.
- `ambience`: `rain` or `empty`. Keep it simple.
- `sfx`: one short sound per scene when the scene has one — `rain`, `birds chirping`, `door creak`, `coins`, `crowd murmur`, `thunder`. Empty only when the scene is genuinely quiet. Sound is never decoration: the sound must match what is happening on screen.

## What makes a moral story work

- One hero, one choice, one moral. Two morals = no moral.
- The hero must LOSE something by doing the right thing (money, time, comfort) — otherwise there is no story.
- Show the choice, do not explain it. The narration says what happened; the moral is one line at the end, not a speech.
- Relatable moments beat big events: a lost wallet, a mother's saved food, a missed bus, an old letter. Small moments, big feelings.
